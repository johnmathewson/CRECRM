# Hobart 2040 Comprehensive Plan — brief for the parcel map strategy

*Source: "It's Hobart's Time 2040 Plan," adopted by the Hobart Common Council Sept 6, 2023 (Res. 2023-14, Plan Commission 5–0). Drafted 2021–2023. Read Nov 2026 to frame the Stewardship CRE parcel map, occupancy layer and opportunity scoring. Page cites are to the chapter PDFs (A = Introduction, B = Vision/FLU, RG = Responsible Growth, CM = Mobility, HN = Healthy Neighborhoods, QL = Quality Lifestyles, WR = Recreation, App = Appendix).*

## 1. What the plan is, and what it missed

Five themes: Healthy Neighborhoods, Responsible Growth, Comprehensive Mobility, Wealth of Recreation, Quality Lifestyles (B p.4). Vision language: "thriving small businesses and clean technology … vibrant, walkable, and mixed-use districts" (B p.3).

The plan assumes a flat city: population 29,219 with a projected **−0.12%/yr to 2027**, households flat at ~11,900, median income rising to $82K (A p.11–12). It never mentions Amazon, data centers or NIPSCO capacity. The closest hooks are "clean technology" in the vision, "communication facilities" as a permitted Employment Center use, a utility corridor mapped west of Grand Blvd, and an INDOT note that a new I-65 interchange "would require a catalyst development increasing traffic" (CM p.10). **Amazon is that catalyst. The plan is the city's wish list written before it had the engine to pay for it.**

## 2. Where the city wants growth (Future Land Use, B p.5–14)

| FLU category | Where | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| **Employment Center** (high-tech, R&D, incubators, storage, "communication facilities," no manufacturing) | 61st Ave / Colorado St block; former drag strip 73rd–81st west of Clay | The Amazon block was already designated for exactly this. Drag strip is the next one. |
| **Industrial** (large parcels, buffers, truck controls; consider banning trucks on Colorado north of US 30) | I-65/Mississippi at Ridge Rd; west of Colorado between 61st and 69th; SR 130/Cleveland; south of US 30 along 83rd; Liverpool/Old Ridge | Supplier, contractor and power-adjacent land. |
| **Commercial Core** ("high-intensity commercial and high-density residential"; medical district "town center"; "potentially funded through Tax Increment Financing") | St. Mary Medical Center area (61st/Grand); downtown Main/3rd | The two places the city will spend TIF on density. |
| **Mixed Neighborhood** (small-lot SF, attached, 2–3 flats; buffer to commercial) | Wedge along Grand Blvd south of 61st to ~73rd; pockets at Old Ridge/Wisconsin, Lake Park Ave, 37th Ave, around downtown | Workforce housing land one mile from the data center. |
| **Multifamily** | 37th Ave/Colonial Dr; US 30/Mississippi; "south of 37th," "Ross Township area," access to Colorado St | Apartment sites the city has pre-blessed. |
| **Auto-oriented Commercial** ("potential to be retrofitted") | US 30, 37th Ave, Ridge Rd | Over-parked lots = infill pads. |
| **Mixed-Use** (commercial first, residential above; 50 ac+ parcels may go light industrial) | South of medical campus along Grand; scattered | Flexible designation worth knowing parcel by parcel. |
| **Future Study Area** ("City should take a leading role") | West of Clay south of 73rd; northwest region | Unplanned land. Whoever brings a plan sets the terms. |

Office was dropped as a land-use category: "physical office spaces are no longer a focus for major employers" (B p.5).

## 3. The city's four named opportunity sites (B p.15–18)

1. **I-65 & Ridge Rd** — "several developable sites … southwest of the interchange," industrial; the stated obstacle is "consolidating sites, which currently have different owners." *Our owner-portfolio tool is built for this.*
2. **37th Ave & Colonial Dr** — Sanitary District land near Strack & Van Til; light industrial, commercial or senior housing.
3. **61st Ave & Wisconsin St** — mixed-use hub for hospital staff, higher-density housing, Wisconsin St extension.
4. **Former drag strip** (west of Clay, US 30) — Employment Center: incubator, small distribution, office; needs 78th Ave + Underwood Dr.

## 4. Market facts the city was working from (2021–22)

- 1,215 businesses; retail is 33% of jobs (4,970), health care 1,686, food/lodging 2,003, **manufacturing only 456**, warehousing 184 (RG p.5).
- Retail: Class A vacancy 3.5%, "smaller retail spaces have become scarce," outlots "remain in demand," obsolete boxes "will likely see more demolitions" (RG p.7).
- Industrial (NWI): Class A vacancy 1.7%, $6.81/SF, record absorption, "supply will continue to lag demand" (RG p.9). "Older industrial properties present an option for redevelopment."
- Office (NWI): Class B "likely to struggle," Class C a value-add target (RG p.8).
- Housing: 12,630 units, 76% single-family, **63% built before 1980, under 3% since 2010**; median value $189K; rents ~$850 with a quarter of renters paying over half their income; **10 rentals on the market** in June 2022; **no owner-occupied independent or assisted senior living in the city** (HN p.2–8).
- Land: 4,152 acres (25%) undeveloped; parks 15.6%; commercial 5.2%; light industrial 2.8% (A p.21–25).
- Developers were already "buying up land, particularly near I-65, U.S. Highway 30, and 61st Avenue" (RG p.9).

## 5. Infrastructure that moves land value (CM, App Thoroughfare Plan)

- **Proposed I-65 interchange at 69th or 73rd Ave**, contingent on a catalyst (CM p.10). Priority-1 new roads: Wisconsin (61st–Clay), Arizona (61st–69th), 79th Ave (SR 51–Mississippi, behind Merrillville Crossings), 83rd Ave (Harms Rd–Colorado/Southlake), County Line Rd (73rd–US 30), Northwind Pkwy to 61st (App p.50). Also 65th Ave Arizona–Colorado, 78th Ave Mississippi–Clay, Iowa St, Colonial Dr north of Ridge (CM p.16). "Private development expected to help fund them."
- Colorado St rebuild 61st–US 30 and the Colorado/US 30 intersection with access-point reconfiguration; 69th Ave widening done; roundabouts at 61st/Marcella, SR 51/SR 130, Hobart Rd/Cleveland.
- Access management: "Direct curb cuts should only be permitted on Local and Collector roadways" (CM p.3). US 30 carries 63,300 vpd near I-65.
- Parking: "most businesses along US Route 30 can be considered 'over-parked'" and that excess "presents an opportunity for infill development" (CM p.8); parking maximums and shared-parking ordinance planned (CM p.19–20).

## 6. Policy levers the city committed to

- Replace B-2 downtown regs, pair with Lake George Historic District guidelines; **reduce reliance on PUD zoning** in favor of form-based districts; use tables instead of cascading uses (RG p.15, HIGH).
- **US 30: parcel-level use mapping, land assemblage review, encourage multistory** (RG p.16; HIGH in CM p.24).
- **Ridge Road: map uses by parcel, review lots for assemblage, allow residential "in segments … that experience the most commercial disinvestment"** (RG p.16).
- Downtown: vacant-storefront activation and tenant matching with the Chamber (RG p.14, HIGH); MF above ground-floor retail "at a density that facilitates meaningful reinvestment," relax downtown parking, **evaluate downtown-periphery parcels for assembly** (HN p.15–16, HIGH).
- A City-maintained **"catalog of commercial and light industrial spaces that can serve as effective business incubators"** (RG p.17).
- Missing-middle zoning: small multi-unit in R-1/R-2, ADUs, no floor-area minimums (HN p.10).
- NRC conservation district for the Lake George watershed and managed lands (QL p.6–7).
- **Strategy 4: a formal "partnership with the commercial real estate community"** — the City will summarize sign rules, non-residential uses and future land use and present at a regular CRE gathering (RG p.12).

## 7. What this means for the parcel map and the opportunity layer

**The plan is our scoring rubric.** The city already wrote, in its own words, what "underutilized" means in Hobart: over-parked US 30 lots, disinvested segments of Ridge Road, empty downtown storefronts, older industrial buildings, multi-owner blocks at I-65/Ridge that need assembling, and downtown-periphery parcels suitable for assembly. Every one of those is computable from data we already have or are about to add:

| Plan signal | Data we have / are adding |
|---|---|
| Over-parked commercial lot | Building footprint area ÷ lot area, by zoning (B-3, US 30 frontage) |
| Current use conflicts with FLU | Property class vs FLU category, already on every parcel |
| Disinvested corridor segment | No active listing + no operating business (occupancy layer) + old improvements + low improvement/land value ratio |
| Assemblage opportunity | Adjacent parcels, different owners, inside an opportunity site or planned-road corridor; owner-portfolio tool |
| Housing-at-core / missing middle | R-1/R-2 parcels near downtown and the medical core with large lots or obsolete structures |
| Planned infrastructure lift | Parcels fronting Wisconsin, Arizona, 65th, 78th, 79th, 83rd extensions and the 69th/73rd interchange study area |

**Where to point the first searches:** the 61st Ave corridor from Colorado to Grand (Employment Center → Commercial Core → Mixed Neighborhood wedge, all within a mile of each other); US 30 over-parked retail for pads and outlots; I-65/Ridge assemblage; downtown vacant storefronts and upper floors; Ridge Road's weakest segments; and the housing gap the plan documents (ten rentals on the market, no senior independent living, sub-3% new stock) against the construction and operations workforce Amazon brings.

**Positioning:** the plan asks for a standing CRE partner and for an incubator-space catalog. The map with an occupancy layer *is* that catalog. Bringing it to the Plan Commission and Redevelopment Commission makes Stewardship the broker the city calls when it maps US 30 and Ridge Road parcel by parcel, which it has committed to doing.

**Caveats:** the plan predates Amazon, carries no fiscal detail (no TIF boundaries, no abatement policy), sets no housing or employment targets, and its appendix holds no survey or demographic tables. Its population projection is stale. Treat it as intent, not forecast.
